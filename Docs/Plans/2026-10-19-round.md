## 2. Wine bar (developer "bar")

**Goal.** A cat sitting on a bar stool orders a glass of wine. A glass appears in front of the bartender, the bartender
swats it with a paw, it slides along the counter to the cat, the cat drinks it, the glass empties.

**Where things are.** The bar is built by `CasinoSceneBuilder.BuildBar` (counter at x −7.8..−2.6, z 5.3; counter top
y ≈ 0.72; bartender `StaffCat` + `NpcCatVisual` at (−5.2, 0.5, 6.0) facing −Z; four `BarStool_1..4` at
x −6.9 + i·1.1, z 4.45). Bar seats are `TerraceTable` "Bar" with 4 seat anchors and seat cameras, built by
`CasinoSceneBuilder.Sea.BuildBarSeats` (check whether the classic scene also gets them; if not, the feature is
sea-scene only and that's fine, say so in the report). Existing bar-top knockable glasses sit at z 4.95.

**Networking.** New `Social/WineBar : NetworkBehaviour` on the same GameObject as the bar's `TerraceTable`, same
authority (master client; the builder must set it up like the tables: `MasterClientObject`, not destroyed when the
authority leaves; copy how `BuildBarSeats` configures the TerraceTable's NetworkObject). Per seat (capacity 4):

- `NetworkArray<byte> OrderIds` (counter, 0 = none)
- `NetworkArray<int> OrderTicks` (`Runner.Tick` when accepted)

Client: `RequestOrder(seat)` → `RPC_Order(byte seat)` to authority. Authority accepts when the seat's occupant is the
RPC source and the seat has no order younger than `ServeSeconds + DrinkSeconds + EmptySeconds`. It increments the id
and stamps the tick. Every client computes `elapsed = (Runner.Tick − OrderTicks[seat]) × Runner.DeltaTime` and plays
the phase from that, so late joiners agree:

| Phase | Time (s) | What |
|---|---|---|
| Pour | 0.0–0.8 | glass appears on the counter in front of the bartender; the wine level rises 0 → full |
| Paw | 0.8–1.3 | bartender `CatReactions.PlaySwipe()`; a `PropClink` at the hit (0.2 s in) |
| Slide | 1.3–2.5 | glass slides along the counter top (z 4.95) to x of the stool, ease-out, small spin; clink on arrival |
| Drink | 2.5–6.5 | the seated cat drinks (§2.3); wine level full → 0 |
| Empty | 6.5–7.5 | empty glass stays a moment; then it disappears and the seat can order again at once (three glasses in ≈ 25 s) |

The player pays **`WinePrice = 20` chips** locally when the RPC is sent (trusted client, like bets); refuse locally with
a toast when the balance is below the price or the seat is still busy. Only the seat's occupant can order.

**2.1 Glass.** The builder generates a `WineGlass` prefab (base disc, thin stem, bowl cylinder with the existing
`PropGlass` material, plus a "Wine" liquid cylinder: deep red `TransparentLit` material, alpha ~0.85, no emission)
and assigns it to the `WineBar`. One instance per seat, created in `Spawned`, inactive when idle. The liquid's Y scale
and position show the level. No physics, no `Knockable`, no `NetworkObject` on the glass.

**2.2 Bartender.** `StaffCat` gets a public `HoldStanding(float seconds)`: it stands up (if sitting) and doesn't sit
back down until then. During Pour..Slide the `WineBar` view calls it and `NpcCatVisual.FaceTowards(seat anchor)`
each frame. The swipe uses the existing paw layer (`CatReactions.PlaySwipe`), nothing new in the animator.

**2.3 Drinking.** Add to the cat animator (in the builder's `BuildCatController`): a bool parameter `Drinking` and a
state `Drink` playing the pack's `licking` clip (looping), reachable from `Sit` when `Drinking` is true, back to `Sit`
when false (0.2 s transitions). `CatAnimatorDriver` gets a `Drinking` property it writes to the Animator. The `WineBar`
view sets it on the seated cat (player cats via `PlayerCat.Find(occupant)`) during the Drink phase. During Drink the
glass does not ride on the cat: it is drawn in from the slide lane (0.4 s) and stands on the counter top where the
cat's snout meets it, at seat anchor + seat forward × `WineBar.DrinkForward` (0.48 m; the counter top's front edge is
0.415 m ahead of the anchor), at counter-top height, and the empty glass stays there.

**2.4 Events.** When the Drink phase ends for the local player's own seat, call `PlayerCat.Local.AddDrink()` (§3).
Chat line when served, from the orderer: `"<name> ordered a glass of wine."` (`PlayerCat.Announce`).

**2.5 HUD.** `TerraceHud` + `Terrace.uxml`: the "Order wine · 20" button and states per the UX spec; **Space** orders on
desktop. Hidden at terrace café tables (only when `TerraceTable.DisplayName == "Bar"`; add a bool `IsBar` to the table
set by the builder rather than comparing strings).

**2.6 Builder anchor.** One line after `BuildBar(hall, materials, npcPrefab);` in `CasinoSceneBuilder.cs` (≈ line 832)
calling your partial's `BuildWineBar(hall, materials)`; it must run after the bar seats exist, so if `BuildBarSeats`
runs later (it's called from the sea builder), hook at the end of `BuildBarSeats` instead and say so.

**Out of scope now (follow-up):** patrons ordering drinks for ambience.

## 3. Drunk state and effects (developer "bar", UX spec §1.1, §1.3)

**3.1 State.** On `PlayerCat`: `[Networked] byte DrinkCount`; owner-only `List<float> _drinkTimes`. `AddDrink()` appends
`Time.time`, recomputes. Each drink expires **6 minutes** after it was drunk (`DrinkDuration`). `DrinkCount` =
unexpired drinks, recomputed once a second by the owner. `IsDrunk => DrinkCount >= DrunkAt (3)`. Events (local, owner):
`DrunkChanged(bool)`. Chat line on becoming drunk: `"<name> has had one too many."`; toast on sobering up:
"You feel steady again." Counts are not saved to the profile (a reload sobers you).

**3.2 Visible to others.** `CatReactions` adds a gentle sway while the cat's `PlayerCat.DrinkCount >= 3` (read on every
client): roll ±4° and yaw ±3° at ~0.7 Hz, amplitude up 20% per extra glass, capped at 5 glasses, blended in over 2 s,
applied in `ApplyModelPose` with the existing hop/rear pose. No speed change.

**3.3 Local effect.** New `CameraRig/TipsyEffect` (MonoBehaviour next to `PlayerCameraRig`): owns a `Volume` with
weight 0 (its own profile created at runtime with `LensDistortion`, `ChromaticAberration`, optional
`ColorAdjustments`), and Cinemachine noise on the follow and override cameras. Values, ramps and the "Tipsy visuals"
pause-menu toggle (PlayerPrefs) come from the UX spec. Nothing runs (volume weight 0, noise amplitude 0, component
disabled) while sober. Mobile: same effect; it is one extra volume blend, no extra passes beyond what URP's uber pass
already does.

**3.4 HUD.** Drink indicator beside the balance and toasts per UX spec (`HudController`, `Hud.uxml`).

**3.5 Pause menu.** "Tipsy visuals" toggle (`MainMenuController` pause panel, same pattern as "Camera auto-follow").

**3.6 Tipsy bonus.** While drunk, a net win (the existing `Local*Result` events, net > 0) pays an extra share by game
(rounded down, minimum 1) into the local balance: **blackjack 20%** (the hard game), **poker 10%** of a won pot's net
(skill game), **baccarat, roulette and slots none** (pure chance). The table lives in one place, `TipsyBonus.RateFor`,
and `TipsyBonus.BonusFor(game, net)` takes the game. Shown with the `balance-gain` label and a toast per the UX
spec. Implemented in `Core/ChipRewards` or a small `Player/TipsyBonus` subscribed to the result events. Trusted client,
like welfare.

## 4. Drunk blackjack totals (developer "bar", UX spec §1.2)

For the **local** player while drunk, their **own** hand labels (`TableOverlay.LabelTable`, hands whose round owner is
the local player) show two candidates: the true total and a decoy. Rules:

- Decoy is a plausible total in 4..21, never the true one, never a bust value when the real hand isn't bust, chosen
  deterministically from a hash of (table id, round id, seat, hand, card count), so it stays put until a card is added.
  The order of the two is from the same hash (the true one isn't always first).
- Soft hands: show the two candidates as plain numbers (e.g. "17 or 21?"), dropping the "Soft" word.
- Bust hands show "Bust" as today. Settled hands (`outcome != None`) show the real total and outcome as today.
- The dealer's label, the HUD phase text, the spectator panel and other players' labels are untouched.
- Presentation per the UX spec.

## 5. Victory cigar (developer "cigar")

**Trigger.** A "huge win" for the local player: a single round or spin whose **gross** payout (what the game hands
back, stake included) is ≥ **500**, in any game, whatever the net or earlier losses (a pushed 500 bet counts). Every
game reports its payout through `ChipRewards.PayRound` (credits the balance; blackjack, baccarat, roulette, both slot
kinds) or `ChipRewards.ReportRound` (poker, whose pot stays in the stack), which raise `ChipRewards.RoundPaid`; a new
game gets the cigar by paying through it. The old top-slot-tier trigger is gone (Top is 50× the bet, so ≥ 500 from a
10-chip bet up). Owner sets `[Networked] int CigarUntilTick = Runner.Tick + 300 s` on `PlayerCat`
(a later huge win extends it). Everyone, including late joiners, renders from the tick.

**Visual.** New `Player/CatCigar` on the cat prefab (the builder adds it to `PlayerCat.prefab` only; NPCs don't win).
On `Spawned`/first use it finds the head bone by walking the skinned hierarchy for a transform whose name contains
"head" (case-insensitive; inspect the FBX bone names with `strings` on `Assets/ThirdParty/BlackCatAnimated/Animations/cat@idle.FBX`
to confirm the name and pick the jaw/muzzle end). Builds the cigar from primitives at runtime (no prefab needed):
a brown cylinder (~6 cm long, 8 mm wide at prefab scale 1.6: tune in cat-local units), a lighter band, and an ember
tip with an emissive material (orange, HDR intensity swings with breathing). Positioned at the side of the mouth,
pointing forward and slightly down. No light (the 32-light cap).

**Smoke.** A `ParticleSystem` child created at runtime, `SoftAdditive` material like the dust motes (`DustMote` material
can be reused with a grey tint via a `MaterialPropertyBlock` or a runtime copy), world-space simulation, max 24 alive
(12 on `Platform.MobileQuality`), size 0.02 → 0.12 over a 2.5 s life, slow rise 0.15 m/s, slight random drift. A
`CatCigar` breathing cycle of 4 s drives it: inhale 1.2 s (emission 0, ember brightens ×3), hold 0.4 s, exhale 1.6 s
(a burst of 6–10 particles then a trickle), rest 0.8 s. The system is disabled entirely while there is no cigar.

**Events.** Chat line from the owner when lit: `"<name> lights a victory cigar."`. Toast per UX spec.

**Builder anchor.** Where `PlayerCat.prefab` is assembled in `CasinoSceneBuilder.cs` (search `PlayerCat.prefab` /
`BuildPlayerPrefab`), one line adding the component. If the prefab build lives in a method you can hook at its end,
do that.

## 6. Table camera views (developer "camera", UX spec §1.4)

**Goal.** Seated players can't see the whole blackjack table. A Camera button (and **V**) cycles views.

**Design.**

- `IGameTable` gains a default member `CinemachineCamera GetOverheadCamera() => null` (C# 9 default interface
  members are fine in this project; check `langVersion` in the csproj first and otherwise add it to every implementer).
- `BlackjackTable` returns an `Overhead` camera built per table by the builder (new partial
  `CasinoSceneBuilder.TableViews.cs`, hooked with one line where the blackjack table's seat cameras are created):
  above the felt's centre, pitched 68–75°, far enough that all five seats, the dealer's cards and the chip circles fit
  on a phone held sideways (16:9 and 19.5:9), with a vertical FOV that keeps the felt filling the frame. Also add it to
  baccarat and poker tables if the hook is the same shape; roulette is out of scope.
- `PlayerCameraRig` gets `TableView` (enum `Seat`, `Wide`, `Overhead`) with `CycleTableView()` and `SetTableView()`
  (as built after the playtest: Wide removed, the View button switches Seat ↔ Overhead; `Overhead` keeps the stored
  value 2 so saved choices survive, and a saved Wide comes back as Seat):
  - Seat: today's seat camera and look.
  - Wide: the seat camera's base pose moved back and up (back 1.1 m, up 0.55 m, pitched down to keep the felt centre
    framed), still with the A/D look. Done at runtime from `_overrideBasePosition`; no new camera.
  - Overhead: the table's overhead camera as a focus-like override (priority above the seat camera); no look input;
    drag/WASD ignored.
  - Transitions use the Cinemachine brain blend (0.8 s ease) already configured. Standing up resets to Seat. The last
    chosen view is remembered per game type in PlayerPrefs (`TableView.Blackjack`) and restored when sitting down.
- HUD: button in `Blackjack.uxml` and `BlackjackHud` per UX spec; key **V** via `GameInput.KeyPressed(Key.V)` (seated
  hotkeys are read in the HUD; keep the same pattern). The touch layout must also show it.

**Follow-ups.**

- The overhead camera was fitted for 16:9 at build time and framed the stools too, so on a phone the cards
  were unreadable. Fixed (Developer 3), with `OverheadFraming` on each overhead camera:
  - It frames the felt only and is fitted at runtime to the screen's aspect.
  - The fitted camera is at most 2.2 m high, and the lens no longer widens.
  - Pinch and the scroll wheel zoom it from 0.7× to 1.3×, remembered per table.
  - On a 4:3 tablet the height cap may still crop the felt's ends.

## 7. Walk threshold (developer "camera", UX spec §1.7)

**Problem.** Any joystick push walks at full `CatMotion.WalkSpeed` (1.4 m/s, which plays the trot) because
`NetworkCharacterController.Move` normalizes the direction. A gentle push should walk slowly.

**Design.**

- `CatMotion`: add `SlowWalkSpeed = 0.5f` (the blend tree already has the `walking` clip at 0.31 × 1.6 ≈ 0.5 m/s).
- `CatMovement.FixedUpdateNetwork`: `maxSpeed` becomes a function of the input magnitude `m` (after the stick's
  deadzone; keyboard gives 1): sprint → `RunSpeed`; else `Mathf.Lerp(SlowWalkSpeed, WalkSpeed, Mathf.InverseLerp(0.15f, 0.9f, m))`.
  Keyboard WASD stays at `WalkSpeed`, Shift runs (unchanged).
- `TouchControls`: stick radius 96, deadzone 0.15, run at ≥ 0.9 of the radius (constants), and the knob/ring visuals
  per the UX spec. `GameInput.ReadMove` must keep the magnitude (it does; `ClampMagnitude(…, 1)` only caps).
- Remote cats: `CatAnimatorDriver` already takes speed from velocity, so the walking clip plays for slow remote cats
  with no change.

## 8. Camera auto-follow (developer "camera")

**Problem.** The follow camera doesn't settle behind the cat. In `PlayerCameraRig.AutoFollowTurn` the turn rate is
`sin(|delta|) × 75°/s`, so it crawls as the angle closes (13°/s at 10°, never arriving), nothing happens at all beyond
115° (walking toward the camera), and the 0.3 m/s velocity gate plus the smoothed anchor add lag. On the web the cursor
isn't locked, so auto-follow is the main way the camera turns; this matters most on phones.

**Design.** Replace the controller:

- Heading from the cat's actual facing (`_target` root yaw) when it's moving (`GameInput.ReadMove()` ≥ deadzone and
  anchor speed ≥ 0.2 m/s), not from the smoothed anchor velocity.
- `delta = DeltaAngle(cameraYaw, heading)`. If `|delta| > 150°` (walking straight at the camera) do nothing: no spin.
- Rate = `clamp(|delta| × Gain, MinRate, MaxRate)` with `Gain = 3/s`, `MinRate = 25°/s`, `MaxRate = 100°/s` (lowered from 140
  to keep the camera from whipping round), i.e. an
  exponential approach that settles within ~1 s and stops when `|delta| < 1°`.
- Keep the 0.6 s hold after a manual look, the pause-menu toggle, and the behaviour while seated (unchanged).
- Verify the cat itself turns toward its move direction promptly: `NetworkCharacterController.Move` rotates at its
  `rotationSpeed`; if it's slow, set it in the builder where the controller is configured (≈ line 641) so a direction
  change completes in ≤ 0.25 s.

## 9. Mobile performance (developer "perf")

**Evidence (from `?debug=perf` on an iPhone at a Yarn Tumble seat, 2026-10-06).** Cool phone: 56 fps, 4.2 ms CPU.
Hot phone: 15 fps, 16.8 ms CPU; half resolution → 40 fps, no extra lights → 24, no slot machines → 20; UI, post,
shadows, FX and HDR made no difference. So a throttled phone is pixel-bound and the per-pixel cost is lighting
(Forward+ on `Mobile_Renderer`, `m_RenderingMode: 2`, 30 realtime lights, additional light shadows on, soft shadows on).
`AutoRenderScale` already steps the render scale down to 0.6 and the slot machine lights are off on Mobile.

**Phase 1 (do now, safe wins).** All on the Mobile quality level / `Mobile_RPAsset` / `Mobile_Renderer` unless noted;
the PC level stays as is.

1. `Mobile_RPAsset`: additional light shadows **off**, soft shadows **off**, main light shadowmap 1024 → 512 with 1
   cascade, shadow distance 50 → 25 (the hall is 22 m; the sea scene's sun is the main light, check what it lights).
2. Light overlap: count, per area of the hall, how many realtime lights reach a pixel (ranges in the builder:
   `PointLight`/`SpotLight` calls). Where more than 3 overlap (slots corner, bar), trim ranges in the builder so the
   worst case is 3, keeping the look (shorter range, slightly higher intensity). Both scenes share these calls; say
   what changed. Lights the sea scene adds (`CasinoSceneBuilder.Sea.cs`, `CatWall.cs`) count too.
3. A Mobile-only runtime trim (`Core/MobileLightTrim`, created like `AutoRenderScale`): on `Platform.MobileQuality`,
   lights the builder tagged as "ambience" (sconces, shelf glow, the cat-wall lamp) get their range × 0.7; lamps over
   games are untouched. The builder tags them by name prefix or a tiny marker component; choose the cheaper.
4. **Adaptive 30 fps**: when `AutoRenderScale` is at its lowest step and the frame rate is still < 45 for 10 s, set
   `Application.targetFrameRate = 30` (steady 30 beats 15–40 judder and cools the phone); go back to 60 (−1) after
   30 s at ≥ 57... but only re-try at the same backoff schedule `AutoRenderScale` uses. Log `[RenderScale] 30 fps`.
   Also a pause-menu setting "Frame rate: Auto / 30" on touch devices (PlayerPrefs), default Auto.
5. CPU: review `TableOverlay.LateUpdate` (labels for every table every frame; skip tables beyond `LabelRange`
   earlier, reuse labels, avoid string building when text is unchanged), `NameplateLayer`, `PatronBrain` (NavMesh
   queries per tick), `WinnerCrowd`. Fix only clear, measurable waste; list the rest.
6. `Platform`/`DebugOverlay`: add the current target frame rate and render scale to the `?debug=1` overlay so the
   user can report what the phone settled on.

**Phase 2 (report only, decision gate).** Baked lighting: hall lamps and sconces as Baked lights with a light-probe
group for the cats and 1–2 realtime "hero" spots over the tables; estimate lightmap size at ASTC on the web build,
the builder changes (`Lightmapping.Bake` like the reflection probe bake), what it costs the desktop look, and the
risk with two scenes. Write it as a half-page in your report; the advisor and the user decide.

You cannot run the phone; reason from code and the evidence above, and make every change switchable from the URL or
the pause menu where it changes the look, so the user can A/B it.

## 10. Build size and Addressables (developer "build", main checkout, Unity MCP allowed)

**Numbers (2026-10-06 web build).** `WebGL.data.unityweb` 18.6 MB and `WebGL_Mobile.data.unityweb` 19.0 MB (gzip),
`WebGL.wasm.unityweb` 12.7 MB (gzip). jsDelivr, which the deploy uses, refuses files over 20 MB, so the data files are

## 10.1 Slot paytable change (Developer 3, as built)

At the user's request the three-reel slot's one-paw line pays the bet back (1×, was 2×; the HUD says "Bet back").
That alone dropped the return to player from 98.6% to 73.6%. Three of a kind was then retuned so a rarer symbol
always pays more (weights fish 36, milk 24, yarn 14, paw 10, mouse 8, 7 4): fish 6×, milk 10×, yarn 12×, paw 24×,
mouse 50×, 7 120× (before: 3, 6, 12, 50, 24, 120, where paw outpaid the rarer mouse). RTP now 94.2%; `SlotRulesTests`
checks 92–96% and the rarity order. About 10% of spins win and 25% hand the bet back.

**After the playtest (Developer 1):** too hard, so a single paw comes up more often: weights fish 30, milk 20, yarn 15,
paw 11, mouse 8, 7 4 (total 88; was 96 with paw 10). Payouts and the rarity order are unchanged. One paw now hands the
bet back on 28.7% of spins (was 25.1%), about 10% still win, and the exact RTP is 96.14% (was 94.2%);
`SlotRulesTests` checks 95.5–97%.

**Bet back more often (Developer 1):** weights fish 22, milk 17, yarn 16, paw 12, mouse 8, 7 5 (total 80) and two paws
pay 3× (was 4×); the three-of-a-kind payouts and the rarity order are unchanged. One paw now hands the bet back on 32.5%
of spins (was 28.7%), two paws come up on 5.7% (was 4.1%), about 10% still win, and the exact RTP is 97.43%;
`SlotRulesTests` checks 95.5–98%.

**Yarn Tumble pays the bet back more often (Developer 1):** weights blue 29, green 22, pink 18, fish 15, mouse 9,
golden paw 7 (was 28, 22, 18, 14, 11, 7) and a refill copies the symbol below 19% of the time (was 25%); payouts and

The cat pack has no swim clip. A workable fake: in water, play the `trotting` clip with the body pitched nose-up ~10°,

### 11.1 Swim R&D (prototype, built)

No water, dock or buoyancy yet. The point is to judge the swim look by eye on dry land.

- **Animator:** a `Swim` state (`CasinoSceneBuilder.Swim.cs`, `BuildSwimState`), bool `Swimming`, the
  `walking` clip at 0.7 speed. It plays whatever the speed, so a still cat treads water. To compare `trotting`
  (quicker, stronger strokes), set `SwimClip = "trotting"` and rebuild the scene. Entered from Locomotion or a

- **Debug switch:** `?swim=1` on the web build starts the local cat swimming; F9 in the editor toggles it.
- **Look at:** whether the slowed walk reads as a paddle with the legs hidden, whether 0.25 m and 8° put the

## 13. NPC count follows the player count (Developer 1, as built)

**Goal.** The hall feels equally busy at any player count: fewer players, more NPCs. GDD §4.5.

- **Numbers.** Today's full set is 10 patrons plus 9 seat cats: five tables on "random" average 1.72
  NPCs each, so 8.6, rounded. TargetCats = 19 + 1 = 20. Budget = clamp(20 − players, 5, 19), where 5 is
  about 25% of 19. Split 10 : 9: 1 player gets 10 + 9, 5 players 8 + 7, 10 players 5 + 5.
- **Authority.** `NpcBudget` is a master-client scene object (`CasinoSceneBuilder.NpcBudget.cs`, both
  casinos). It networks the patron count, the seat scale in percent, the player count it last saw and
  a 10 s settle timer. The first master sets the budget at once; after that, a join or leave restarts
  the timer and the budget changes when it runs out.
- **Patrons.** `PatronBrain` checks `NpcBudget.PatronWanted(sibling index)` each tick on the master.
  - An unwanted cat drops its spot or watch and walks to `CasinoDoor.Inside`. On arrival it sets the
    networked `Away`, which hides its body on every client.
  - A wanted `Away` cat reappears at the entrance and picks a spot.
  - `IsLeaving` keeps paws and `WinnerCrowd` off leaving cats. A new master carries on from the
    networked state.
- **Seat cats.** `NpcSeating.Plan` scales the rolled target when a table re-rolls. Nothing is removed
  mid-round, and the per-seat `SeatNpcRests` are untouched. No closed-seat mask was needed: the scaled
  target already says how many seats a table refills, and it lives in the table's networked `NpcCrew`.
- **Not tested in play.** Look at:
  - With 1 player, the hall is as before.
  - With a second client, or with `PlayerCat.All` raised, patrons walk out about 10 s after the joins
    and tables thin out within about a minute.
  - Patrons walk back in after players leave.
